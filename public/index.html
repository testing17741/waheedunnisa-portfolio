Doctoral Investigation

Microbial Waste Valorization & Pigment Extraction

Converting millions of tonnes of discarded marigold temple flowers into stable bio-pigments and sustainable aquaculture feeds.

Pillar 01

Ensilage Fermentation of Temple Flowers

Marigold (Tagetes erecta) waste discarded in waterways causes severe oxygen depletion. Anaerobic fermentation creates an acidic, protective microenvironment, blocking oxidative degradation of high-value carotenoids.

Biomass Stabilization
Pillar 02

Microbial Cell-Wall Lysis (Enzymatic)

Endogenous microflora and added consortia synthesize cellulases and pectinases. These enzymes break down rigid lignocellulosic petal membranes, yielding pure xanthophyll (lutein) without hazardous petrochemical solvents.

Green Bioprocessing
Pillar 03

Catfish Tissue Coloration & Feed Nutrition

Testing extracted natural pigments as feed supplements for freshwater catfish (Clarias batrachus). Results measure tissue carotenoid deposition and serum antioxidant protection against synthetic dyes.

Aquaculture Application
Microbiology Workbench

Characterized Microflora Strains

Key microbial species isolated, cultured, and analyzed during the anaerobic fermentation of marigold biomass.

Lactobacillus plantarum

Lactic Acid Bacterium • Acidifier

Ferments water-soluble carbohydrates present in floral petals into lactic acid. Drops substrate pH below 4.2 rapidly, inhibiting spoilage organisms and preserving delicate xanthophyll rings from oxidation.

Rhizopus nigricans

Filamentous Fungus • Cellulolytic Agent

Produces extracellular cellulases and pectinolytic enzymes. Deconstructs outer plant cellulose walls, facilitating non-thermal release of intracellular carotenoid esters into the extraction matrix.

Flavobacterium IIb

Gram-Negative Bacterium • Synergist

Participates in the secondary microbial succession of ensilage, contributing organic acid by-products and stabilizing carotenoid pigment precursors throughout the 21-day fermentation cycle.

Acinetobacter anitratus

Oxidative Microorganism • Bioconversion

Demonstrates metabolic adaptability during early ensilage phases, aiding complex substrate breakdown and the formation of GC-MS verified phytobiotic compounds.
